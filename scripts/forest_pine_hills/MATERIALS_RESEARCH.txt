Implementation:
- Custom local PineHills.mdl uses the bundled OmniSurfaceBase, not downloaded
  MDL. Authoring sets only materials and primvars on this new stage; there are
  no global render settings or physics/sensor callbacks.
- Ground colour/roughness/normal maps tile in world metres and blend between
  three projection axes. 2 m / 2048 pixels gives 0.9766 mm source texels,
  compared with the previous 12.2 mm terrain-wide atlas. Actual resolved detail
  still depends on camera distance, source photography and RTX filtering.
- Smooth vertex weights combine slope, local curvature, relative height,
  canopy density, broad spatial variation and a ragged needle/root strip.
  Four layers are needles, moss, soil and rock. Soil is a darker/desaturated
  derivative of the verified needle-litter scan, not a separate soil scan.
  Exposed roots are geometry authored by the terrain/scatter module.
- Ground uses verified forrest_ground_03 and forest_leaves_02 PBR maps plus
  rock_moss_set_01 rock maps. Model textures are reused at their original 2K
  resolution. Original texture copies are checked against the CC0 manifest
  MD5; local shader resources are generated by this repository.
- Bark/logs have an additional world-projected normal layer at a 0.18 m tile;
  moss has a small sheen lobe. Fine detail is mip-filtered by RTX with projected
  size. An explicit camera-distance shader fade was not used because the GUI
  and four sensor cameras need a consistent material without callbacks.
- Needles and fern fronds use thin-walled diffuse transmission, not glass-like
  specular refraction or volumetric subsurface random walks. Geometry remains
  opaque to RTX lidar; alpha trimming is handled by the established geometry
  pass. Two-sided meshes and thin_walled shading are both authored.
- Per-instance variation was changed after measured renderer costs. The initial
  design used distinct inherited root tint/wetness primvars. Those preserved
  USD's 66 prototypes / 9,817,653 prototype triangles / 100 prototype meshes /
  4,606 native instances even when blocked or equalized, but that alone did not
  establish RTX memory sharing. The first run exhausted 95 GB during geometry
  allocation and also contained an MDL compile error, so the entire failure
  cannot be attributed conclusively to primvars alone.
  A corrected 8-pair palette rendered at diagnostic splat RTF 0.664 and 17.6 GB;
  the parent measured the no-root-variation candidate at about 0.779 in that setup.
  Final choice: hash each object's existing world origin using
  state::transform_point(coordinate_object, coordinate_world, float3(0)).
  This gives stable per-instance hue, value and wetness; one shared prototype
  pineHillsVariationStrength primvar controls hue/value strength through
  data_lookup_uniform_float. Installed support_definitions.mdl lines190-205
  forwards that exact API. There are no unique inherited tint/wetness payloads.
  Parent's correctly composed iteration 06 compiled cleanly and used 8.3 GB;
  diagnostic RTF 0.781 versus 0.769 with variation disabled was within noise.
  The visual effect is deliberately subtle. These are diagnostic comparisons
  with an additional comparison camera, not the final required 60-second RTF.
  This replaces the originally requested per-instance primvar payloads with
  renderer transforms plus a shared primvar to retain the real-time budget.
  Scale/shape variation belongs to terrain/scatter authoring.
- Reference-ground iteration: trail core soil weight raised from 0.38 to 0.64,
  trail moss suppression from 82% to 96%, moss tint from (.67,.91,.49) to
  (.48,.58,.29). The source scans and world tiling remain unchanged; the goal
  is a rough brown/gray root track between darker olive moss and fern edges.
  A later explicit Ground diffuse_gain of 0.65 scales only the blended diffuse
  albedo, without changing camera exposure, trunks or fern materials. Both
  gain and moss tint are authored as USD inputs for inspection and ablation.
  The ground-gain-1 render comparison must establish its visual benefit;
  performance cost is not inferred from this constant shader multiplier.
- Root-sheet audit: pine_roots scans have valid UVs and opaque double-sided
  geometry, not missing alpha. Their dark scanned soil skirts sat above the
  terrain and exposed angular borders. The two closest foreground patches had
  median gaps of 1.3/1.9 cm and boundary P90 gaps of 4.8/5.9 cm. Boundary-based
  seating belongs to scatter geometry; brightening the root material would
  not fix those silhouettes. No texture or shader defect was found in them.
- Nonvisual presets are attached to bound visual Material prims. Ground face
  subsets select the dominant layer's preset while visual blending stays
  smooth. Foliage/moss use leaf_grass; these are physically based generic
  classifications, not measured pine/moss reflectance calibration.
- Ground subset renderer correction: the shared builder's three floor_*
  subsets originally remained active with zero face indices and old OmniPBR
  bindings. Offline USD queries correctly resolved the four new Ground
  materials and an exact face partition, but RTX still rendered the old
  texture regions. The old leafy_grass texture was visible even though the
  new Ground shader never samples it. Changing Ground gain from .65 to .01
  then changed full-frame mean RGB by less than .3/255 (iteration 10).
  A 715-byte diagnostic layer deactivating only the three old subsets fixed
  the quilt while retaining all four new nonvisual material groups.
  In iteration 11, baseline mean RGB was 76.8/81.8/71.1 and gain .01 changed
  it to 54.7/60.5/51.7, confirming shader consumption. The exact internal RTX
  material-slot defect is inferred; no claim about its implementation is made.
  Final authoring deactivates superseded subsets instead of merely clearing
  their indices, preserves their original data, and authors no active empty
  replacement subsets. Repeated authoring explicitly reactivates replacements.
  Earlier ground-detail/gain comparisons are invalid evidence for Ground
  appearance or cost. Final acceptance RTF must use the corrected bindings.
- No shader displacement or tessellation: terrain relief and roots use real
  geometry with matching collision/GT, avoiding visual-only ground offsets.
