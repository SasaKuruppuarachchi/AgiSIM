FOREST_PINE_HILLS — terrain and agipix_auto handover

World coordinates are metres, Z up, with the take-off pad at world Z=0.
Vehicle spawn is (0,0,0.07). These are world heights, not AGL or PX4 NED.
The terrain and its triangle-mesh collider share the same 401 x 401 vertices
and 320,000 triangles at 0.25 m spacing over X/Y [-50,50] m.
Seed 2026 ground heights: -4.349 to 9.087 m; relief 13.435 m. Maximum actual
triangle slope 28.5 degrees (gradient 0.543); 95th percentile 17.56 degrees.
The nominal flat pad radius is 3.8 m, blending to the hills by radius 11 m.
The verification explicitly checks a completely flat radius 3 m and no mature
stem surface within 4 m. Roots/logs/rocks and low branches still need avoidance.

Suggested first terrain-following scan: X/Y [-12,12] m, then expand to
[-22,22] m after checking the live terrain estimate and obstacle avoidance.
The larger 44 x44 m rectangle has ground Z=-3.874..5.421 m. Allowing a 4 m
route margin, the [-26,26] rectangle has ground Z=-4.122..7.011 m.
Use short polyline segments (at most 5 m) and the existing forest planner
configuration. The drawing is a coverage corridor, not a collision-free path.
The trees and understory require the planner's lateral relocation/avoidance.

The bounds Z values are absolute world heights even when waypoint Z is AGL.
Do not reuse FOREST_FI's narrow absolute vertical bounds or a constant world-Z
flight plane. The default terrain_max_slope 0.4 is below this world's steepest
ground; 0.56 covers the authored mesh, but its online estimation behaviour in
this forest still needs a dedicated autonomy test. The ideal-GT-aided PX4
clearing flight recorded separately is not such a terrain-following test.

Monitor /drone0/auto/status: area_scan, terrain_conf, terrain_ground, agl,
band_lo/band_hi, relocation and waypoint_index. Inspect the ground estimate
before expanding into the eastern ridge/gully or dense low spruce cover.
The GT files are evaluation data; the online terrain estimator should continue
to use its lidar observations rather than reading the GT heightfield.
